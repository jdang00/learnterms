# Negative Exception

Template label: `negative.exception`

## Purpose

The learner must identify a false, NOT, EXCEPT, least likely, or not indicated option.

## Reasoning Order Fit

- Order depends on the underlying cognitive task.
- Use `first` for simple false/NOT recall, `second` for concept boundaries, and `third` for case-based exceptions.

## Do Not Use When

A positive framing would test the same skill more cleanly.

## Common Stem Patterns

- Which is NOT [true/indicated]?
- All are true EXCEPT...
- Which is least likely?

## Anatomy Of This Template

- Stem highlights the negative task.
- Correct answer is the exception.
- Distractors are true members of the category.
- Rationale states the positive rule and why the exception is outside it.

## Considerations

- Avoid double negatives.
- Use negative questions only when the boundary is educationally meaningful.
- Capitalize NOT or EXCEPT in final generated stems.

## Production Examples

Each example keeps only the content and semantics needed to understand the template. Source wording is preserved where useful, but new generated questions should use clean wording.

### Example 1: Chapter 7

#### Question Content

- Context: OPT6023 Ocular Disease II: Glaucoma / Chapter 7
- Format: multiple choice, single-best-answer
- Stem: Which of the following medications would NOT be indicated in cases of normal tension glaucoma?
- Options:
  - Brimonidine
  - Timolol [correct]
  - Rhopressa
  - Dorzolamide
  - Betaxolol
- Rationale: ++Timolol is a beta-blocker medication that, although it performs well with IOP reduction, it also induces systemic hypotension. This is not a great idea in a patient with NTG, where the damage has likely manifested from a vascular insufficiency to the optic nerve head. ++You might be thinking, "Betaxolol is a beta-blocker, too?" And you are right! However, it, along with dorzolamide and Rhopressa, have been shown to actually increase perfusion to the ONH.

### Example 6: Binocular Vision, Part 2

#### Question Content

- Context: OPT5215 Vision Science III: Motility / Binocular Vision, Part 2
- Format: matching
- Stem: Classify each prompt with whether or not it is characteristic of harmonious or unharmonious correspondence.
- Correct pairings:
  - prompt:Angle S is 0 -> answer:Harmonious
  - prompt:Angle S is smaller than Angle H -> answer:Unharmonious
  - prompt:Angle H does not equal Angle A -> answer:Unharmonious
  - prompt:Angle A - Angle H = 0 -> answer:Harmonious
- Rationale: In harmonious AC, the angle of anomaly (A) is equal to the objective angle of strabismus (H). This means the subjective angle (S) is 0 since the eccentric retinal point is perceived as "straight ahead.". In unharmonious AC, the subjective angle S is smaller than the objective angle (H), meaning there is a discrepancy between Angle A and Angle H. If H and A don't agree, you have unharmonious AC.

### Example 7: Binocular Vision, Part 1

#### Question Content

- Context: OPT5215 Vision Science III: Motility / Binocular Vision, Part 1
- Format: matching
- Stem: Consider an object being off to the left in your visual field. With your head straight ahead (i.e. not facing the object), you perform a levoversion to bifoveally fixate the target. Match the following to what they conclude.
- Correct pairings:
  - prompt:Oculocentric Localization (OS) -> answer:Object is straight ahead
  - prompt:Egocentric localization (cyclopean) -> answer:Object is to your left
- Rationale: Oculocentric localization is confined to the foveal location of the object, meaning that if your fovea is fixated on an object, the RETINA sends a signal back to the brain saying the object is "centered". Egocentric localization takes it a step further by "adding" information about where the eyes are pointed (via efference copy, proprioception) to determine that the object is located to the left of the cyclopean eye.

### Example 8: Systemic Disease and Prescribing for Pedatrics

#### Question Content

- Context: OPT5233 Pediatrics / Systemic Disease and Prescribing for Pedatrics
- Format: multiple choice, multi-select
- Stem: Which of the following systemic diseases found commonly in the pediatric population that are NOT genetically transmitted?
- Options:
  - Alport Syndrome
  - Ehlers-Danlos
  - Pseudoxanthoma Elasticum
  - Ear infections [correct]
  - Eczema [correct]
  - Fabry's Disease
  - Acute lymphoblastic leukemia [correct]
  - Marfan's Syndrome
  - Neurofibromatosis
  - Osteogenesis Imperfecta
  - Sickle Cell
  - Sturge-Weber Syndrome [correct]
  - Tay-Sachs
  - Wilson's Disease
- Rationale: If you can remember that Ear infections/tubes, eczema, acute lymphoblastic leukemia, and Sturge-Weber syndrome are NOT genetic, you know that the rest are! Alport Syndrome is a rare, x-linked genetic abnormality that affects the synthesis of collagen IV, leading to kidney failure, sensorineural deafness, and yellowish white flecks in the perimacular area of the fundus. Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome is a genetic disorder caused by defects in collagen biosynthesis that can result in hypermobile joints, blue sclera, and ocular manifestations such as angioid streaks or lens subluxation. Fabry's Disease is an x-linked recessive genetic condition caused by a lysosomal storage abnormality of the enzyme ceramide trihexoside, resulting in decreased sweating, chronic pain, and whorl-like opacities of the cornea. Marfan Syndrome is an autosomal dominant connective tissue disorder affecting fibrillin-1 that typically presents in tall, thin patients with hyperflexible joints and ocular signs like ectopia lentis. Neurofibromatosis is a genetic disorder that triggers the growth of tumors throughout the nervous system and is often identified by light brown cafe-au-lait spots on the skin or growths on the iris. Osteogenesis Imperfecta is a genetic bone disorder known as "brittle bone disease" that causes frequent fractures, progressive hearing loss, and a characteristic blue appearance of the sclera. Sickle Cell is a hereditary disease characterized by abnormally shaped red blood cells that can lead to painful bone episodes and retinal complications such as sea-fan neovascularization and angioid streaks. Tay-Sachs is an autosomal recessive genetic disorder where lipids accumulate in the nerve cells of the brain and spinal cord, causing premature death and a classic cherry red spot in the macula. Wilson's Disease is an autosomal recessive disorder involving the abnormal deposition of copper in various organs, which frequently produces a Kayser-Fleischer ring at the limbus of the cornea.
