# Diagnosis Case

Template label: `diagnosis.case`

## Purpose

The learner must infer a diagnosis from a compact clinical presentation.

## Reasoning Order Fit

- Usually `third` order.
- Use `second` only for very brief test interpretation without a full patient-case frame.

## Do Not Use When

The item is only asking for a memorized diagnostic feature.

## Common Stem Patterns

- A patient presents with [findings]. What is the most likely diagnosis?
- Given this history and exam, which condition best explains the presentation?

## Anatomy Of This Template

- Stem includes signal findings and relevant negatives.
- Correct answer is the best diagnosis.
- Distractors are common look-alikes.
- Rationale maps clues to diagnosis.

## Considerations

- Every case detail should earn its place.
- Use age, timeline, symptoms, and exam data only when they change the diagnosis.
- If management is included, make sure the diagnosis is still clear.

## Production Examples

Each example keeps only the content and semantics needed to understand the template. Source wording is preserved where useful, but new generated questions should use clean wording.

### Example 1: Trauma to the Anterior Segment

#### Question Content

- Context: OPT5273 Ocular Disease I: Anterior Segment / Trauma to the Anterior Segment
- Format: multiple choice, multi-select
- Stem: Your 34 YOM patient recently took a football to the side of the dome, near the left temple area. He notes some moderate to severe pain behind the eye. You notice limited EOM motility in the left eye with FROM in the right eye. His pressures were OD: 17 mmHg OS 32 mmHg. You conduct the red cap test to check for any gross optic nerve issues. He states the redness is a 35% decrease in the left eye vs the right eye. You also notice a mild afferent pupillary defect in the left eye. You send him for a CT scan and get the following results. What is your diagnosis and treatment?
- Options:
  - Orbital Blow-Out Fracture
  - Orbial Inflammatory Pseudotumor
  - Traumatic Retrobulbar Hemorrhage [correct]
  - Thyroid Eye Disease
  - Orbital Cellulitis
  - Artificial Tears PRN
  - Keflex 500 mg P.O. [correct]
  - Bandage Contact Lens
  - Lateral Canthotomy [correct]
  - Timolol QID [correct]
- Rationale: This presentation aligns with a case of traumatic retrobulbar hemorrhage. Treatments include a lateral canthotomy to release the pressure from the blood buildup within the orbit and Oral antibiotics (cya for potential orbital cellulitis). Also, you should probably address the IOP spike with IOP control meds, such as Timolol or Diamox.

  - Resistant Bacterial Keratitis
  - HSV Epithelial Keratitis

Treatment will likely include a swift phone call to the nearest corneal specialist to get this condition under control because, in the eye, fungi are NOT FUN GUYS ! ### Example 8: History

- Format: multiple choice, multi-select
- Stem: Your 45 YOF patient comes to you complaining of a swollen red left eye and some notable tearing that started about 3 days ago. She has no history of punctal plugs. What is your most likely diagnosis and treatment?
- Options:
  - Dacryocystitis
  - Dacryoadenitis
  - Canaliculitis [correct]
  - Punctitis
  - Dacryostenosis
  - Keflex 500 mg [correct]
  - Polytrim QID [correct]
  - Prednisone 80mg
  - Dilation and Irrigation
- Rationale: The following picture illustrates a "pouting puncta", which is a pathognomonic sign for canaliculitis. Furthermore, the lower grade inflammation also aligns with this diagnosis over something like dacryocystitis (more tissue that could get inflamed --> more swelling). This condition can be treated equally well with a topical or oral antibiotic, as the canaliculi are 'open' to the ocular surface, which makes a topical antibiotic a viable option. In general, D&I is not indicated for infection conditions (this case is LIKELY infectious because it is not based on a blockage from something like a punctal plug, which may indicate it would be more of an inflammatory condition).
