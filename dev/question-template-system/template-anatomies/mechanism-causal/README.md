# Mechanism Causal

Template label: `mechanism.causal`

## Purpose

The learner must know why a finding, drug effect, experiment, or clinical outcome happens.

## Reasoning Order Fit

- Usually `second` order.
- Use `third` only when the mechanism must be integrated with diagnosis or management in a case.

## Do Not Use When

The source only supports a surface association.

## Common Stem Patterns

- Which mechanism explains [phenomenon]?
- Why does [cause] lead to [outcome]?
- Which pathway is responsible for [effect]?

## Anatomy Of This Template

- Stem asks for why or how, not just what.
- Correct answer contains a causal link.
- Distractors are wrong causal stories.
- Rationale traces cause -> intermediate -> outcome.

## Considerations

- Mechanism rationales should be longer than recall rationales.
- Make the intermediate step explicit.
- Do not invent causal detail beyond the source.

## Production Examples

Each example keeps only the content and semantics needed to understand the template. Source wording is preserved where useful, but new generated questions should use clean wording.

### Example 1: Binocular Vision, Part 2

#### Question Content

- Context: OPT5215 Vision Science III: Motility / Binocular Vision, Part 2
- Format: multiple choice, multi-select
- Stem: In Hubel & Wiesel's Occlusion Kittens experiment, which of the following BEST explains what happened regarding when the kittens were deprived of their binocular visual input?
- Options:
  - If the kittens were visually deprived during the critical period only and allowed binocular vision after the end of that period, their visual cortex would slowly return to normal
  - If the kittens were visually deprived during the critical period only and allowed binocular vision after the end of that period, their visual cortex would never recover normal binocularity [correct]
  - Enucleation helped return performance of the deprived eye after the critical period
  - Enucleation did not help return performance of the deprived eye after the critical period [correct]
- Rationale: Hubel and Wiesel demonstrated that the visual cortex possesses a critical period of high plasticity where neural connections are permanently shaped by sensory experience. If normal binocular input is not established during this specific developmental window, the structural changes in the ocular dominance columns become permanent and cannot be reversed by subsequent visual experience or surgical interventions like enucleation.

### Example 2: Binocular Vision, Part 2

#### Question Content

- Context: OPT5215 Vision Science III: Motility / Binocular Vision, Part 2
- Format: true/false
- Stem: A person with eccentric fixation does not necessarily have anomalous correspondence, but a person with anomalous correspondence has eccentric fixation.
- Options:
  - True [correct]
  - False
- Rationale: True! Eccentric fixation (EF) and anomalous correspondence (AC) are related but distinct adaptations. EF is a monocular condition where the deviated eye uses a non-foveal point for fixation instead of the fovea, and this can occur simply from motor habituation without any binocular involvement. AC, on the other hand, is a binocular sensory adaptation where the brain remaps retinotopic connections to pair the fixing eye's fovea with a non-foveal point in the deviating eye, allowing both eyes to share a common visual direction and avoid suppression. Because AC establishes a new "pseudo-fovea" in the deviating eye under binocular conditions, that same point will naturally be used for fixation under monocular conditions, meaning AC always brings EF along with it. However, since EF can develop from simple motor habituation alone without any binocular remapping, a patient can have EF without AC. This is why the statement is true: EF does not necessarily imply AC, but AC always implies EF.

### Example 3: Binocular Vision, Part 1

#### Question Content

- Context: OPT5215 Vision Science III: Motility / Binocular Vision, Part 1
- Format: multiple choice, multi-select
- Stem: Assuming a normal individual with an expected response, which color will seem closer? Further away?
- Options:
  - Blue Closer
  - Blue Further [correct]
  - Red Further
  - Red Closer [correct]
- Rationale: This is due to the idea that shorter wavelengths are refracted MORE than longer wavelengths. So, the blue letters will strike nasal to the fovea, which will project it to the temporal field = UNCROSSED DISPARITY --> Perceived FURTHER AWAY. The red letters do the exact opposite: They strike temporal to the fovea, thus it seems as if it came from the nasal field = CROSSED disparity --> Perceived CLOSER Overall, Yellow light (555nm) is roughly the middle of the spectrum and will land on the fovea with the blue and red straddling the fovea on either side.
