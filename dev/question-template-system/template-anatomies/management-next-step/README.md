# Management Next Step

Template label: `management.next_step`

## Purpose

The learner must choose treatment, adjustment, referral, monitoring, counseling, or follow-up.

## Reasoning Order Fit

- Usually `third` order.
- Use `second` only for a direct one-step action after a simple finding.

## Do Not Use When

The item only asks for a diagnosis or isolated fact.

## Common Stem Patterns

- What is the most appropriate next step?
- Which treatment would be most effective?
- What adjustment should be made?

## Anatomy Of This Template

- Stem includes the decision context.
- Correct answer is actionable.
- Distractors are actions that would be reasonable if a detail changed.
- Rationale explains why the action fits the constraints.

## Considerations

- Include allergies, severity, age, pregnancy, stage, or prior treatment when relevant.
- Do not ask unsafe management without source support.
- Use multi-select when diagnosis and treatment are both expected.

## Production Examples

Each example keeps only the content and semantics needed to understand the template. Source wording is preserved where useful, but new generated questions should use clean wording.

Treatments include a lateral canthotomy to release the pressure from the blood buildup within the orbit and Oral antibiotics (cya for potential orbital cellulitis). Treatment will likely include a swift phone call to the nearest corneal specialist to get this condition under control because, in the eye, fungi are NOT FUN GUYS ! ### Example 5: Chapter 10-11

#### Question Content

- Context: OPT6023 Ocular Disease II: Glaucoma / Chapter 10-11
- Format: multiple choice, multi-select
- Stem: You observe the following in a 79 YOM patient with an IOP of 29 mmHg (assume no phacodonesis). What are some possible treatment options for this patient?
- Options:
  - PredForte [correct]
  - Durysta (bimatoprost)
  - Homatropine [correct]
  - Timolol [correct]
  - Laser peripheral iridotomy
  - Travatan Z (travoprost)
  - Extracapsular cataract extraction [correct]
  - SLT
- Rationale: This is an autoimmune inflammatory response against liberated lens proteins secondary to a hypermature cataract. Let's consider the treatment in a step-by-step manner: Inflammation: Calm it with a topical steroid => PredForte (DOC) Leaky blood aqueous barrier: Stabilize it with a topical cycloplegic => Homatropine (DOC) Be sure there is no phacodonesis (lens jiggle). If there is, cycloplegic subjects patient to potential lens subluxation. Elevated IOP: Lower it with AQUEOUS SUPPRESSANTS, ONLY => Timolol, brimonidine, dorzolamide, brinzolamide Hypermature cataract: Remove the cataract => Extracapsular cataract extraction (used for 'advanced' cataracts) AVOID PGs in this case because it will only ADD to the inflammation.

### Example 12: Ocular Side Effects of Systemic Drugs

#### Question Content

- Context: OPT5203 Ocular Pharmacology / Ocular Side Effects of Systemic Drugs
- Format: multiple choice, single-best-answer
- Stem: A patient presents with complaints of objects in his periphery seem to catch him off guard, as if they just seem to pop into view once he views said objects centrally. He takes medications to help treat his seizure disorder. You perform a Humphrey VF 30-2, and it yields the following results. Which medication is most likely to yield this kind of damage?
- Options:
  - Fingolimod
  - Digitoxin
  - Isotretinoin
  - Vigabatrin [correct]
  - Ethambutol
- Rationale: This visual field loss is largely indicative of vigabatrin (Sabril). Sabril inhibits metabolism of GABA, an inhibitory neurotransmitter that is meant to help quell the intense neural firing that is responsible for seizures. GABA also has the ability to inhibit neurotransmission in the Retinal periphery, and when patients take Sabril, they are subject to GABA neurotoxicity that can cause irreversible and progressive peripheral VF loss.
