# Output quality inspection

These are the assistant's inspections of actual generated items against the captured lecture excerpts, not a clinician-graded accuracy benchmark. Automated pass counts must not be interpreted as accuracy. Exact outputs, citations, assigned excerpts and review verdicts are retained locally in `tmp/studio-sol-terra/`.

The first inspection covered every generated question in the four matched 10-question Sol/Terra runs (40 items), followed by the locally accepted Luna low/medium comparison items and selected 15-question outputs. The rubric asks whether the key is uniquely justified, each rationale claim follows from its assigned evidence, alternatives are plausible, explanations teach a relationship, and wording stands alone. Duplicate screening is a separate mechanical gate. Minor wording issues and clinically consequential ambiguity are not equivalent.

The emergency example is a problem with the question's uniqueness and justification, rather than proof that every intermittent angle-closure presentation has identical urgency. The stem lacks the clinical detail to dismiss angle closure because a lecture labels GCA as urgent. Acute angle-closure attacks require urgent care; see the [AAO Primary Angle-Closure Disease Preferred Practice Pattern](https://www.aaojournal.org/article/S0161-6420%2825%2900816-4/fulltext). Source omission does not establish that a competing answer is false.

## Reviewer limitations

- The verbose Luna medium reviewer accepted the emergency-label question in Sol none and Terra none, despite instructions not to infer falsity from source omissions. It also approved conspicuous irrelevant distractors in Sol low and Luna low. An independent API call is not an independent clinical gold standard.
- The 10-question Luna low run received eight automated passes after duplicate filtering, yet one accepted item paired calcific emboli with tear-film abnormality and cholesterol emboli with vitreous debris. Another imaging question used ocular ultrasonography as a supposed definitive carotid test. These are teaching-quality defects that a pass count hides.
- The 10-question Luna medium run had supported explanations, but an MRI question contrasted brain imaging with orbital radiography and electroretinography, and some prose retained source framing. More thinking alone did not eliminate these problems.
- Some reviewers reject reasonable explanatory inferences as unsupported, while accepting substantially weaker alternatives elsewhere. Their rejection counts are not calibrated quality scores either.
- The compact-review experiment uses a strengthened, shared prompt across reviewer models, including an explicit general guard against treating an urgency label as an exclusive distinction. Because this guard was added after observing the cardiovascular errors, performance on that example is an adaptive regression check, not held-out proof. The second lecture is a separate check on generalization.

## Fifteen-question and second-lecture checks

- Terra low, cardiovascular batches of five, `learn-pages-7-0`: alternatives include a preserved cherry-red spot, an isolated macular cherry-red spot and a peripheral cherry-red spot. These are overlapping or implausible distinctions. The verbose reviewer passed the item. The same run's carotid-dissection timing question preserved the disease qualifier, an improvement over the broad stroke-risk generalization seen in earlier experiments, but it still relies on the lecture's unverified statistic.
- Sol none, cardiovascular batches of five, repeated the emergency-label ambiguity at `learn-pages-2-1`; verbose screening passed it again. Two BRAO questions also repeated much of the same field-loss/acuity teaching content despite passing the mechanical duplicate gate.
- Terra low's retinal generation handled the telangiectasia vessel list more faithfully than Luna's sampled retinal generation, and several MacTel explanations use useful type-to-mechanism and imaging distinctions. It returned 12/15 with explicit abstentions on sparse material, at 5.79 cents before checking. This is a real positive quality observation, not sufficient evidence of a general model advantage.
- Luna medium, retinal batches of five, `learn-pages-3-1`: the source lists terminal arterioles, capillaries, microaneurysms and venules, but the question chooses terminal arterioles and its rationale denies source support for capillaries and venules. Those are competing answer options. The verbose reviewer passed it. The compact Luna medium reviewer rejected it as unsupported but still returned only index 0 as defensible; the compact Terra low reviewer passed it. Thus even the more expensive low-thinking checker failed a concrete source-reading/ambiguity test.
- Both retinal generators convert captions of mild/moderate Coats examples into stronger general severity distinctions. This deserves curator review; a captioned example alone is not a validated disease-staging rule.
- The fastest hybrid (Luna medium drafting, Terra none compact review) accepted every one of its 11 local cardiovascular candidates. Inspection still finds classification/list-recall questions, a tautological BRAO definition, invented or implausible embolus alternatives, and carotid-ultrasound distractors that instead describe cardiac or retinal testing. The 18.1-second, 2.48-cent result therefore does not establish the requested quality.

All four compact reviewers caught the known emergency-label question after the prompt guard was added. Luna medium did so for 0.29 cents, Terra none for 1.69 cents, Terra low for 2.19 cents and Sol none for 3.28 cents for that nine-candidate review. All four still passed the weak gradual bilateral-loss distractor in the BRAO item. Switching reviewer model did not resolve the core grading inconsistency.

## Interpretation

Low thinking looked preferable to none for avoiding the specific high-impact ambiguity in the first matched Sol/Terra samples. It did not establish an overall accuracy advantage, and weak distractors persisted. Good rationales were present in all three model families; the larger model name did not guarantee better teaching. Any proposed default must satisfy complete-set count, cost, latency and manual quality together, rather than selecting the fastest inexpensive run or the highest automated pass count.
